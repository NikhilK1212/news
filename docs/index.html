  • Father-of-two carpenter, 62, gets life-saving transplant thanks to record-breaking new NHS technique that could save 750 lives a year
    Published: 11:24 EDT, 17 October 2026 | Updated: 16:10 EDT, 17 October 2026 A British man has become the first to benefit from a pioneering lung transplant operation in an NHS breakthrough which could save up to 750 lives a year. The extraordinary achievement saw Joe McConnell, 62 – who was dying and reliant on oxygen – receive donor lungs which might ordinarily have been rejected because of their condition. But instead, they were transported between three different hospitals and kept alive and 'breathing' in a specially-designed box to allow doctors to monitor their function and boost the chances of his operation being a success. The specialist hospital at the centre of the pilot study, Royal Papworth in Cambridge, said it was 'the furthest perfused lungs have ever travelled for transplantation outside North America, creating a new UK and European record'. At the moment, many lungs offered up for donation are rejected before being removed because they fall into a clinical 'grey area' where specialists cannot be certain they will be suitable to be transplanted into seriously ill patients.
