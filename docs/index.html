  • What California’s Dem-On-Dem Races Reveal About The Future Of Democrats
    As Democrats revel in their epic slate of wins in this year’s midterms, many are already hailing the results as a sign that the progressive wing of the party is finally in the ascendant. But in deep-blue California, neither progressives nor moderates walked away with a clean sweep in the Democrat-on-Democrat races. California is unique in the nation not just because it is the most populous state but also because of its top-two primary system, in which the two candidates that receive the most support in the primary — regardless of party — advance to the general election. As a result, eight House contests in the state wound up being races between two Democrats this fall. Similarly, the Los Angeles mayoral race was an intraparty showdown, though it is technically nonpartisan.

  • US lawmakers launch probe into Lutnick’s actions amid Nvidia scandal
    Letter alleges possible conflict of interest in Lutnick not adding Megaspeed to US trade blacklist. Democratic lawmakers have opened a probe into whether United States Commerce Secretary Howard Lutnick used his position to help Cantor Fitzgerald, an investment firm controlled by his two sons, avoid financial losses amid the scandal involving Jensen Huang at artificial intelligence chip giant Nvidia. In the five-page letter dated November 17, House Democrats notified Brandon Lutnick, chairman of Cantor Fitzgerald and Howard Lutnick’s son, that they are requesting information to ascertain whether “any actions or decisions by Secretary Lutnick concerning export controls, enforcement measures, or national security policies may have been improperly influenced by Cantor Fitzgerald’s holdings or business interests”. The letter was authored by Jamie Raskin of Maryland, who is the ranking member of the House Judiciary Committee, Gregory Meeks of New York and the ranking member of the House Foreign Affairs Committee, and Sydney Kamlager-Dove of California. It asks if the company’s financial exposure influenced Lutnick’s decision to keep Megaspeed off the US Entity List, a trade blacklist.
