  • Volkswagen strikes deal with Israel investment firm to turn car plant into defense facility | CNN Business
    A Volkswagen car plant in northwestern Germany is to be converted to defense production, the company said Monday, as Europe’s biggest economy ramps up military spending amid a continent-wide push to rearm. Volkswagen said in a statement that it had agreed to sell its Osnabrück car plant to the State of Lower Saxony and Aurelius Capital, an investment firm headquartered in Tel Aviv. The local government and Aurelius, which would be the majority owner, plan to work with Rafael Advanced Defense Systems, one of the companies behind Israel’s Iron Dome. ” The deal will also preserve jobs at the site, where vehicle production is due to end next year, offering a blueprint for other Volkswagen factories at risk of closure. The automaker has warned that four German plants may need to shutter in the 2030s as it battles weak demand, rising costs and intense competition from China’s electric vehicle makers.

  • EU Pledges Greenland Investments Amid Trump's Push For Control
    NUUK, Sept 6 (Reuters) - European Commission President Ursula von der Leyen unveiled plans on Monday for €200 million ($232 million) of EU investments in Greenland and closer ties with the Danish Arctic territory, which President Donald Trump has insisted should belong to the United States. Von der Leyen arrived in Greenland on Sunday for a two-day visit serving as a show of support for the governments of Greenland and Denmark, which have strongly rebuffed Trump’s campaign to acquire the world’s largest island. The head of the 27-nation European Union’s executive body said the EU money would focus on sectors including critical minerals, satellite communications and renewable energy in Greenland, a semi-autonomous part of the Danish realm. “The EU stands in full solidarity with the Kingdom of Denmark and the people of Greenland,” von der Leyen told a news conference. ” Von der Leyen’s visit reflects the increasing geopolitical importance of the Arctic to major powers, such as the United States, Russia, China and Europe, as melting ice opens new shipping routes to make the region’s mineral wealth more accessible.
