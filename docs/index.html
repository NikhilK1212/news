  • Moderna, Merck breakthrough could usher in wave of cancer vaccines
    Merck and Moderna’s success with a personalized cancer vaccine designed to keep melanoma at bay opens a ​new avenue of treatment that doctors hope will work against many types of tumors. Unlike chemotherapy, which kills both healthy and cancerous cells, or immunotherapy, which revs up ‌the immune system, personalized cancer vaccines train the immune system to specifically target mutations found only on a person’s tumor. Merck and Moderna said on Wednesday their vaccine helped prevent the return and spread of disease in a large trial involving more than 1,000 melanoma patients. They had localized tumors that were removed surgically, but had a high risk of recurrence. Moderna expects that thousands of melanoma patients could benefit within the first few years of approval.

  • Mobilization Migration: Is There An Uptick In Russians Fleeing Amid Fears Of A New Draft?
    Sergei Rasokhin says he spent two years in hiding before finally escaping military service in Russia and recently making it to safety in Armenia. The country took in many Russians dodging the draft in 2022-23 and now, amid growing fears of a new mobilization in Russia to boost a military depleted by the war on Ukraine, there are some indications that the numbers of Russians coming to Armenia are again rising. There have also been recent signs of this in other countries that saw an influx of Russians in 2022-23, but the evidence is patchy and sometimes conflicting. In any case, if the Kremlin decides to announce a new wave of mobilization, it may wait until after parliamentary elections next month -- meaning those hoping to avoid it still have time. Rasokhin told his story to RFE/RL’s Armenian Service from the safety of Yerevan, home to a thriving community of Russians who have fled the increasingly repressive environment in their homeland under President Vladimir Putin in recent years.
